# Retention and visitor erasure

Choose how long to keep event history and aggregates, and understand what visitor erasure removes. These decisions determine which historical reports remain available.

## Choose retention by purpose

| Tier | Library default | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw events | 90 days | Ad-hoc queries, segments, funnels, erasure, and backfills |
| Minute rollups | 2 days | Fine-grained recent reports when enabled |
| Hour rollups | 400 days | Hourly history and finer report boundaries |
| Day rollups | Forever | Long-range calendar reports |

The default enabled rollup tiers are Hour and Day. A retention setting for Minute does not enable that tier. Null retention keeps the selected tier indefinitely.

## Understand expiry

Queries hide expired records according to retention cutoffs. Scheduled maintenance, hourly by default, reclaims physical storage through partition removal and merges.

Cutoffs move in whole UTC days, or whole hours for retention shorter than a day. Physical cleanup can finish later than logical expiry.

Indexes follow the retention of their source data. Increasing retention or adding an index cannot restore records already removed.

## Erase an identified visitor

<import-codeblock src="./visitor-erasure.cs"/>

Supply the same explicit visitor ID and site used for collection. Erasure removes retained raw events, their configured dimension-index copies, and active session state. Its return value counts deleted raw events.

Already aggregated rollups are not recomputed. Historical aggregate reports can therefore retain contributions after the corresponding detailed event records are erased.

## Plan historical analysis

Keep enough raw history for the segments, funnels, and index backfills you need. Daily totals can preserve long-range trends without preserving ordered individual behavior.

Daily anonymous rotation does not provide a reversible account lookup after its salts are destroyed. Use explicit application identity when retained events must be associated with a known account's erasure request.
